# VAEL — How the platform works

**For:** Client review  
**Date:** 6 October 2026  
**What this covers:** The public website, Vael In, and Vael Out as they work today.

VAEL is one platform. People arrive through the same website, create an account in the same City, and use the same signed-in home: matches, opportunities, districts, handshakes, and community.

Vael In and Vael Out are two roles inside that City. They are kept apart on purpose. Each role answers a different question, walks a different setup, and sees a different next step. One side offers availability. The other side looks for it. The platform connects those two actions. It does not ask both people to fill in the same form.

---

## 1. One platform

The public site and the signed-in City are the same product.

**On the website**, anyone can look around without an account:

- Home
- Explore Districts
- Projects
- Community
- Sign in
- Go Visible

**After sign-in**, the person lands in the City dashboard. The navigation is the same shell for every account:

- Home
- Opportunities
- Districts
- Matches
- Handshakes
- Community

What changes is the role. The greeting, the status, the primary button, the profile questions, and the opportunities list all follow whether this account is Vael In or Vael Out.

A person chooses one role for the account they are in. Moving to the other role is a separate sign-in, so the current profile is not rewritten into the opposite job.

---

## 2. The two roles

| | Vael In | Vael Out |
|---|---|---|
| The question | “I’m available for work.” | “I’m looking for someone.” |
| What they offer | Skills, services, and current availability | A need, a role, or a project |
| What the other side sees | A person who can be hired | A person who is hiring, or a project that needs doing |
| Dashboard greeting | Hey Vael In | Hey Vael Out |
| Status | Visible, with time remaining | Vaeled Out, with time remaining |
| Main action once set up | Stay visible, and meet people who are hiring | Create an Opportunity |
| Opportunities in the sidebar | Work they can respond to | Projects they have created |

Visibility lasts 24 hours. When that window ends, the person can Vael again if they are still available or still hiring.

---

## 3. How someone enters

Go Visible asks one question: **What are you here to do?**

Three choices:

1. **Vael In** — I’m available for work. I offer my skills, services, or availability to be matched.
2. **Vael Out** — I’m looking for someone. I need someone available for a role or opportunity.
3. **Opportunities** — Post a job. This opens the home-or-business board immediately. It does not ask for an account or a sign-in first. Choosing “For my home” continues into the opportunity form. “For my business” stays Coming soon.

If the person is not signed in, the next screen is account creation, and the choice they just made is carried into setup. If they are already signed in, they continue in the flow that matches that choice.

That first choice is what keeps the experience short. Steps that belong to the other role are skipped.

---

## 4. Vael In

Vael In is the person who can do the work.

**Setup**

1. Create an account.
2. Choose Vael In.
3. Welcome, then identity: handle, how they will use VAEL, and the district their matches come from.
4. Build the profile: name, photo, location, headline, discipline, skills, experience.
5. Add what they can do: the kind of work, how they take part, working preference, and current availability.
6. Review every section. Each section can be edited before they go visible.
7. Go visible. They arrive on the Vael In dashboard.

**On the dashboard**

- Greeting: Hey Vael In.
- Status: Visible, with the hours left in the 24-hour window.
- Home shows profile completion, their district, and matches.
- Matches are people who fit what they offer and who are hiring.
- Opportunities opens the list of live work they can respond to.
- A handshake starts when they accept a request, or when they respond to someone on the board.

The profile they built is the thing the other side matches against. Vael Out never fills in “what I can do.” Vael In does.

---

## 5. Vael Out

Vael Out is the person who needs someone.

**Setup**

Vael Out has its own steps. It does not reuse the Vael In “what I can do” form.

1. Create an account.
2. Choose Vael Out.
3. What I need — who is hiring, the role, and a description of the work.
4. Who I’m looking for — discipline, category, and skills.
5. Location — where the work is based.
6. Requirements — skills or certifications, if any.
7. Timing — when they need someone.
8. Additional details — tools, engagement, budget range, links, documents, photo. These are optional.
9. Review. Each section can be edited, then they post.
10. They arrive on the Vael Out dashboard.

**On the dashboard**

- Greeting: Hey Vael Out.
- Status: Vaeled Out, with the hours left in the window.
- Home shows people who match what they are hiring.
- The primary button is **Create an Opportunity**.
- Opportunities in the sidebar opens the projects this account has created.
- A handshake starts when they request one from a match.

The request they posted is what Vael In is matched to. Vael In never fills in “who I’m hiring.” Vael Out does.

---

## 6. Creating an opportunity

Create an Opportunity is a Vael Out action. It is a second flow, after the account is already Vaeled Out. It describes a specific job, separate from the hiring profile.

**First screen.** A board asks: **Is this for your home or your business?**

- **For my home** — repairs, renovations, and new builds. This continues.
- **For my business** — shown on the same board, marked Coming soon. It does not open a form yet.
- **Back** returns to the Vael Out dashboard.

**After “For my home,”** the job is collected one step at a time:

1. Category — kitchen, bathroom, flooring, roofing, and the other work types.
2. Description.
3. Photos and video. Optional.
4. Location — city and postal code.
5. Timing — as soon as possible, within 30 days, 1–3 months, or just planning.
6. Budget — a range, or “not sure.”
7. Review. Each section sits in its own card and can be edited without redoing the whole form.
8. Verify email and phone, then publish.

Back is available on every step, including the home-or-business board. Editing from review returns to that section and then back to review.

A published home project is what a Vael In member can find under Opportunities. The person who posted it manages that project from the Vael Out side.

---

## 7. How the two sides meet

The platform is the place they meet. The roles stay distinct.

```
Website
  └─ Go Visible
       ├─ Vael In  → available profile → visible for 24 hours
       ├─ Vael Out → hiring profile   → visible for 24 hours
       └─ Opportunities → home or business → home project form, before any account

Vael Out posts a need or a home project
        ↓
Vael In sees a match, or an opportunity they can respond to
        ↓
One side requests a handshake. The other accepts.
        ↓
They can message while the window is open.
```

- **Matches** on Vael In are people hiring. Matches on Vael Out are people available to hire.
- **Opportunities** on Vael In are jobs to take. Opportunities on Vael Out are jobs this account created.
- **Handshakes** hold both incoming and outgoing requests. If nobody acts before the 24-hour VAEL ends, the other person is released back to the board. Either side can Vael again later if the other person is still available.

Community, districts, and messages sit in the same City for both roles. The words on those screens follow the role: a Vael Out update reads as hiring, and a Vael In update reads as availability.

---

## 8. Why the flows are separate

Putting both jobs on one screen would ask each person to answer questions that are not theirs.

- Vael In is asked what they can do, and whether they are available now.
- Vael Out is asked who they need, and what the work is.
- A home project is asked as its own short sequence, only after Vael Out chooses Create an Opportunity, and only after they choose “For my home.”

Same account system. Same dashboard. Same match and handshake mechanics. Different questions, in the order that matches the role.
